# Design: making Campus Customs feel like a real Yale storefront (Problem 10)

**Idea:** the shop should feel like walking down to 57 Broadway at dusk, with stone, lamplight, and a
friendly bulldog behind the counter, not like a template. Built on Problem 9's base (campus photo,
"Yale at dusk" colors, clean product photos), Problem 10 adds the touches below.

| Area | What we changed | Why it helps people stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Fonts and hierarchy** | Cormorant Garamond for headlines and the wordmark, Inter for everything you read. Small-caps kickers, italic section numerals (I.–IV.), and tabular figures so prices line up. | Reads like a Yale publication, not a bargain bin. A clear order (headline, why, action) gets shoppers to the products faster. |
| **Color: a living sky** | The site's sky colors follow the shopper's own time of day: dawn pink, daytime blue, sunset gold, night indigo. This tints the hero photo, the announcement bar, the chat header, and Dan's button. The hero greets you ("Good evening from Campus Customs") and a caption names the moment ("Lamplight on Old Campus"). Preview any phase with `?sky=dawn` / `day` / `dusk` / `night`. | A small surprise that makes the site feel alive and personal, so people linger. It keeps the calm blue-and-sand palette and never changes the product colors. |
| **Motion** | Sections and product cards fade up as they scroll into view (staggered), the hero photo drifts slowly, and the chat panel springs open. All of it switches off for visitors who prefer reduced motion. | Gentle movement guides the eye down the page to the next thing to shop, without slowing anyone down. |
| **Storefront details** | A slow ticker in the top bar with real store facts (officially licensed, 57 Broadway, ships to 30+ countries, live stock). A new **Shop by residential college** shelf with shield-shaped monogram crests for the 11 colleges in the catalogue. | The ticker builds trust with true facts. The college shelf speaks to how Yalies actually shop: "Where's *my* college?" is one click. |
| **Product presentation** | Cards: an **"Almost gone"** badge from live stock (25 or fewer left; 8 products today), color swatches next to the price, and on hover the photo zooms toward the chest print with "Look closer →". Product page: a **magnifier lens** that follows the mouse over the print, swatches on the color pills, and green/brick stock labels per size. | Shoppers want to see the crest or print up close before buying, and the lens does that without leaving the page. An honest scarcity cue (real numbers, never invented) nudges people who are on the fence. |
| **Chat feel: meet Dan** | The assistant is now **Dan**, a bulldog drawn entirely in HTML/CSS (wrinkles, jowls, underbite, a gold "Y" collar tag, blinking eyes). He sits on the chat button ("Ask Dan"), in the header, and beside each reply. **Suggestion chips** change with the page (home: "What's good for game day?", "A gift for a Yale dad"; product page: "Is this in my size?", "What size should I get?", "Show me similar styles"). A one-time nudge appears after 7 s ("Need a size or a gift idea? Ask Dan."). Product pages add an **"Ask Dan about this one"** box that opens the chat and asks for you. | A face and a name make the assistant approachable, so more people ask. Chips remove the "what do I type?" moment. One-tap questions turn sizing doubts, a common reason people hesitate to buy clothes online, into an answer with live stock. |
| **Fall on Old Campus** | **String lights** run down both sides of every page, each bulb flickering at its own pace; they glow brighter at dusk and night (tied to the living sky). **Autumn leaves** drift down the side gutters, never over the content, and **pile up at the bottom of every page**. Scrolling down reveals the pile: "You found the leaf pile. 41 leaves have fallen while you browsed", with Dan's picks. | Movement at the edges makes the store feel alive without competing with products, and the pile is a small reward for scrolling the whole page, past more products. |
| **Leaves that hint "you might like this"** | The site remembers how long a shopper lingers on each item (hovering a card, reading its page; stored only in their own browser). Now and then a leaf settles in the corner of a card **similar to what they lingered on** (same kind, college, sport, or color) with a small "Picked for you" tag. Hover or tap the card and the leaf is **brushed away**. At most 3 resting leaves, always in a corner, so products are never hidden. | A gentle, playful nudge toward relevant items, discovered while browsing rather than pushed in a pop-up. |
| **Product page: what to pair it with** | **Complete the look**: an outfit built around the item, with an inner layer and an outer layer chosen for matching college/sport/family theme and colors that go together, plus a styling note and the whole-look price (e.g. Benjamin Franklin quarter-zip + Benjamin Franklin tee + Benjamin Franklin fleece: "Wear the tee under this quarter-zip…, and add the fleece on cold New Haven nights." $202). **{Kind} in other colors** and **More {kind} like this** rows below. All rule-based from the live catalogue (in stock only, no AI call), so they're instant. | Outfits sell more than single pieces, and other colors and similar styles keep a shopper who's unsure about one item from leaving. |

**Kept honest:** badges, ticker facts, and chat answers all come from the database or the store
facts in `output/harness.md`. Nothing on the site invents a sale, a shipping promise, or a stock
number.

**Where to look:** Home (sky greeting, ticker, college shelf, scroll reveals), any product grid
(badges, swatches, hover zoom, leaves settling on cards), any product page (magnifier, "Ask Dan about
this one", Complete the look, other colors), the bottom of any page (the leaf pile), the sides (string
lights; best on a laptop-width window), and the chat button in the corner. All motion turns off for
visitors who prefer reduced motion, and nothing decorative blocks a click.
